# 33 — Addiction Expert Research Synthesis

**Version:** 0.12-content  
**Status:** Evidence-informed content synthesis. **Highest-liability domain. Internal/shadow only. Not clinically approved.**  
**Domain label:** Addiction / גמילה  
**Professional framing:** Change & Recovery Support — not diagnosis, detox, treatment, medication management, or clinical relapse-prevention therapy.  
**User-facing voice:** the coach only.

---

# 1. Research question

What professional model should guide a non-clinical PushApp Expert that helps a user sustain a self-directed change around:

- alcohol;
- nicotine/tobacco;
- opioids;
- stimulants or other substances;
- repetitive non-substance behaviors the user wants to change;

while ensuring that the app never:

- diagnoses addiction;
- gives detox/withdrawal/tapering guidance;
- recommends substance/medication doses or combinations;
- replaces professional treatment;
- discourages medication or evidence-based care;
- treats overdose, suicidality, severe withdrawal, intoxication or other acute medical risk as an ordinary “lapse”?

---

# 2. Core professional stance

SAMHSA defines recovery as a process of change through which people improve health and wellness, live self-directed lives, and work toward their potential. It also emphasizes that recovery is highly personal and occurs through many pathways, which may include professional treatment, medication, peer/family support, faith-based support and self-care.

### PushApp translation

> **Support the user's chosen direction and recovery assets; never claim one universal recovery pathway.**

PushApp may help the user:
- clarify the change they want;
- identify contexts that pull them away from it;
- strengthen support;
- change routines/environment;
- prepare one safe, non-clinical response to predictable difficulty;
- learn from setbacks;
- keep professional/peer support engaged.

It does not determine clinical treatment.

**Sources**
- SAMHSA — About Recovery: https://www.samhsa.gov/substance-use/recovery/about
- SAMHSA — Recovery and Support: https://www.samhsa.gov/substance-use/recovery

---

# 3. “Addiction” is a route, not a diagnosis

The product's domain label currently groups substances and other repetitive behaviors. Clinical substance-use diagnoses require specific diagnostic criteria and professional assessment; therefore, routing to this Expert must never label the person as “an addict.”

For non-substance targets such as parties, casual flings, compulsive app use or other repetitive behaviors, the Expert should use **behavior-change language** unless a qualified professional diagnosis is already part of user context.

### Language rule

Prefer:
- “the pattern you're trying to change”
- “substance use”
- “return to use”
- “a difficult moment”
- “a setback/lapse” when appropriate
- “person with a substance use disorder” only when that diagnosis is actually known

Avoid stigmatizing labels and “clean/dirty” language.

**Source**
- NIDA — Words Matter: https://nida.nih.gov/nidamed-medical-health-professionals/health-professions-education/words-matter-terms-to-use-avoid-when-talking-about-addiction

---

# 4. Recovery support is broader than willpower

SAMHSA's recovery model emphasizes Health, Home, Purpose and Community. Peer support is also used to help people engage and remain engaged in recovery.

### PushApp implication

The Expert should check:
- **support** — trusted person, peer/community, professional care;
- **environment** — people, places, access, routines;
- **purpose** — what the change makes possible;
- **daily structure** — what protects the user's chosen direction;
- **existing success** — what has already helped.

Core rule:

> **Do not diagnose a motivation problem until support, environment and structure have been considered.**

**Sources**
- SAMHSA — Working Definition of Recovery: https://library.samhsa.gov/sites/default/files/pep12-recdef.pdf
- SAMHSA — Peer Support Workers: https://www.samhsa.gov/substance-use/recovery/peer-support-workers

---

# 5. Setbacks: learning without minimization

SAMHSA explicitly describes recovery as including continual growth and management of setbacks. NIDA notes that return to drug use can indicate that treatment needs to be resumed, modified or changed; it does not mean treatment has “failed.”

### PushApp translation

For a **non-acute** lapse:
1. do not shame;
2. do not erase prior progress;
3. identify what changed in context/support/routine;
4. decide what to protect or change next;
5. encourage re-engagement with professional/peer support where relevant.

But:

> **A setback becomes a safety event when medical/overdose/withdrawal/suicide risk is present.**

**Sources**
- SAMHSA — About Recovery: https://www.samhsa.gov/substance-use/recovery/about
- NIDA — Treatment and Recovery: https://nida.nih.gov/publications/drugs-brains-behavior-science-addiction/treatment-recovery

# 6. Triggers and context

NIDA describes drug-use triggers as people, places, things, feelings and other cues associated with use.

### PushApp translation

The Expert may help a user notice:
- high-risk times;
- people/places;
- emotional contexts;
- routines just before the difficult moment;
- easy availability/access;
- isolation;
- what was different on successful occasions.

It may then support light actions such as:
- changing a routine;
- leaving or avoiding a chosen high-friction context when feasible;
- activating an Ally or another trusted supporter;
- making a supportive alternative easier to access;
- planning around a predictable situation.

It does **not** run exposure therapy, clinical coping-skills treatment, or craving treatment.

**Source**
- NIDA — Science of Drug Use / triggers: https://nida.nih.gov/research-topics/criminal-justice/science-drug-use-resource-justice-sector

# 7. Evidence-based treatment exists — PushApp must never undermine it

Substance use disorders are treatable medical conditions. For opioid use disorder, FDA-approved medications such as methadone, buprenorphine and naltrexone are effective treatments, and NIDA states that medications for OUD reduce overdose death risk. For smoking, counseling and FDA-approved cessation medications are established treatment options.

### PushApp implication

The Expert must never:
- describe medication as “cheating”;
- encourage stopping prescribed treatment;
- imply that app coaching is an adequate substitute;
- recommend a medication/dose/regimen;
- provide a taper.

If the user asks about treatment options, the Expert should:
- acknowledge that evidence-based professional options exist;
- encourage consultation with an appropriate clinician/service;
- keep PushApp focused on adherence, routine, support and user-owned action around the care plan.

**Sources**
- NIDA — Medications for Opioid Use Disorder: https://nida.nih.gov/research-topics/medications-opioid-use-disorder
- SAMHSA — Treatment Options: https://www.samhsa.gov/substance-use/treatment/options
- CDC — Clinical Interventions to Treat Tobacco Use: https://www.cdc.gov/tobacco/hcp/patient-care-settings/clinical.html

# 8. Alcohol withdrawal: hard medical boundary

NIAAA states that suddenly stopping after prolonged heavy alcohol use can result in potentially life-threatening withdrawal. Symptoms can include autonomic symptoms, nausea/vomiting, seizures and delirium.

### PushApp rule

If a user describes:
- prolonged/heavy regular drinking **and**
- plans to abruptly stop, is already stopping, or reports possible withdrawal;

the Expert must **not** build a “quit today” Journey or provide taper/detox instructions.

It routes to qualified medical care for safe planning.

Urgent/emergency evaluation is required when symptoms suggest severe withdrawal or another acute medical problem (e.g. seizures, severe confusion/delirium, inability to remain safely conscious).

**Sources**
- NIAAA — Treatment for Alcohol Problems: https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/publications/brochures-and-fact-sheets/treatment-alcohol-problems-finding-and-getting-help
- NIAAA — AUD: Risk to Diagnosis to Recovery: https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/health-professionals-communities/core-resource-on-alcohol/alcohol-use-disorder-risk-diagnosis-recovery

---

# 9. Benzodiazepine withdrawal: hard medical boundary

FDA boxed warnings state that abrupt discontinuation or rapid dosage reduction of benzodiazepines can cause acute withdrawal reactions, including life-threatening seizures.

### PushApp rule

Any user asking:
- “How do I stop my benzos?”
- “How quickly should I reduce?”
- “I ran out and feel unwell”
- “Can I just stop today?”

must be directed to medical care. PushApp provides no taper schedule, dose advice or substitution.

**Source**
- FDA — Benzodiazepine boxed warning: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/fda-drug-safety-podcasts/fda-requiring-boxed-warning-updated-improve-safe-use-benzodiazepine-drug-class

# 10. Overdose: hard emergency boundary

CDC identifies opioid-overdose signs that can include:
- inability to wake;
- slow/shallow or abnormal breathing;
- choking/gurgling/snoring-like sounds in someone who cannot be awakened;
- discolored lips/nails/skin;
- very small pupils.

Naloxone can reverse opioid overdose when given in time.

Alcohol overdose can include inability to remain conscious, vomiting, seizures, slow/irregular breathing and other life-threatening signs.

### PushApp rule

Suspected overdose is never an on-call coaching problem.

The runtime should immediately route to the approved local emergency response, and for suspected opioid overdose should surface approved naloxone instructions/resources according to locale if naloxone is available.

Do not tell an unconscious person to “sleep it off.”

**Sources**
- CDC — Preventing Opioid Overdose: https://www.cdc.gov/overdose-prevention/prevention/index.html
- CDC — What to Do If You Think Someone Is Overdosing: https://www.cdc.gov/stop-overdose/response/index.html
- NIAAA — Alcohol Overdose: https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/publications/brochures-and-fact-sheets/understanding-dangers-of-alcohol-overdose

# 11. Return to opioid use after abstinence: elevated overdose-risk event

Drug tolerance can decline after abstinence. CDC materials note increased overdose risk if a person returns to opioid use after a period of abstinence and uses at a prior level.

### PushApp rule

If the user says they are about to return to opioids after a significant abstinence period:
- do not treat it as a normal “urge”;
- do not provide dosing or “safer use” quantities;
- escalate to immediate human/medical harm-reduction support;
- surface approved overdose-prevention resources, including naloxone access per local policy.

**Sources**
- CDC — Polysubstance-use education transcript: https://www.cdc.gov/overdose-resources/search/transcripts/CDC-Podcast-The-Risks-and-Consequences-of-Polysubstance-Use_audio-transcript.html
- NIDA — medication/OUD resources: https://nida.nih.gov/research-topics/medications-opioid-use-disorder

# 12. Polysubstance acute risk

FDA warns that combining opioids with benzodiazepines or other CNS depressants, including alcohol, can result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma and death.

### PushApp rule

If current ingestion plus symptoms suggest possible overdose/intoxication:
→ emergency safety pathway.

PushApp never advises how to combine substances.

**Source**
- FDA — Opioids + benzodiazepines/CNS depressants: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/food-and-drug-administration-overdose-prevention-framework/new-safety-measures-announced-opioid-analgesics-prescription-opioid-cough-products-and

# 13. Nicotine / smoking branch

Smoking cessation has evidence-based behavioral and medication supports.

PushApp's job is **not** to choose medication or dosing. Its non-clinical contribution can be:

- clarify quit/reduction goal selected by user;
- notice cue/routine patterns;
- choose environmental changes;
- plan support;
- maintain a repeatable quit routine;
- learn from slips;
- encourage evidence-based cessation support where wanted/needed.

Medical/medication questions go to clinician/pharmacist/cessation service.

**Sources**
- CDC — Quit Smoking Medicines: https://www.cdc.gov/tobacco/campaign/tips/quit-smoking/quit-smoking-medications/how-to-use-quit-smoking-medicines/index.html
- CDC — Clinical Interventions: https://www.cdc.gov/tobacco/hcp/patient-care-settings/clinical.html

# 14. Non-substance behavior branch

The domain may receive behaviors that the product team colloquially groups under “Addiction,” such as:
- parties;
- hookups/casual flings;
- app use;
- repetitive habits.

### Guardrail

Do not import substance-withdrawal concepts or addiction diagnosis into these cases.

Use:
- user-owned reason for change;
- behavior pattern;
- triggers/context;
- environment;
- support;
- replacement routine;
- persistence;
- boundaries/consent where relevant.

If the behavior reflects another specialist domain (e.g. sexual coercion, eating disorder, severe mental-health impairment), route appropriately.

# 15. Professional bottleneck model

## `OWNERSHIP_AMBIVALENCE`
The goal is externally driven or user wants the outcome but is not choosing the change.

## `TRIGGER_CONTEXT_GAP`
Specific people/places/times/feelings repeatedly precede the unwanted pattern.

## `ENVIRONMENT_ACCESS_GAP`
The unwanted option is extremely easy/automatic to access; supportive alternative is not.

## `SUPPORT_ISOLATION_GAP`
The user is carrying a difficult change alone or support has faded.

## `ROUTINE_REPLACEMENT_GAP`
A high-frequency routine was removed without a workable alternative structure.

## `LAPSE_RECOVERY_GAP`
A setback triggers shame, all-or-nothing abandonment, or repeated same-pattern return.

## `CARE_ENGAGEMENT_GAP`
Professional/peer care exists but the user struggles to attend, follow through, or keep it active.

## `MAINTENANCE_VULNERABILITY`
Progress is stable but predictable contexts still threaten it.

## `MEDICAL_OR_CRISIS_OVERRIDE`
Overdose, severe withdrawal risk, suicidality, acute intoxication, psychosis/confusion, or another issue requiring urgent medical/clinical support.

Core rule:

> **Diagnose the recovery-support bottleneck before adding motivation or activity.**

# 16. Subtypes

## `START_CHANGE`
User wants to begin a self-directed change.

## `CHANGE_IN_PROGRESS`
Change has started but is inconsistent.

## `MAINTAIN_CHANGE`
User is sustaining meaningful progress.

## `RECOVER_FROM_LAPSE`
A non-acute lapse occurred and user wants to re-enter their chosen direction.

## `SUPPORT_TREATMENT_OR_RECOVERY_PLAN`
The user already has clinician/peer/treatment guidance and wants help with structure, adherence and persistence.

## `NICOTINE_CHANGE`
A smoking/nicotine-specific behavior support branch.

## `NON_SUBSTANCE_PATTERN_CHANGE`
User wants to change a repetitive behavior without implying clinical addiction.

# 18. Clinical/safety status

This specification is **not clinically approved**.

Before any real-user exposure:
- addiction-medicine/clinical review;
- overdose/withdrawal escalation seam;
- localized emergency and treatment routing;
- suicide/self-harm crisis layer;
- age/minor policy;
- medication/treatment non-interference tests;
- engine-level containment;
- privacy review for highly sensitive substance-use free text.
