# 27 — Relationships / Loneliness Expert Research Synthesis

**Version:** 0.11-content  
**Status:** Evidence-informed content synthesis. High-liability / mental-health-adjacent. Requires formal clinical/safety review before real-user exposure.  
**Domain:** Relationships / Loneliness — social connection, dating opportunity, existing-relationship maintenance, assertiveness/boundaries.  
**User-facing voice:** the coach only.

---

# 1. Research question

What professional model should guide a non-clinical PushApp expert whose job is to help the meta-agent move a user from:

> “I want more/better connection”

to:

> a user-owned, respectful, safe, real-world pattern of connection behavior,

without becoming a therapist, matchmaker, couples counselor, diagnostic tool, or pickup coach?

---

# 2. Strongest conclusions

## 2.1 Loneliness is not simply “too few people”

The WHO Commission on Social Connection (2025) distinguishes social connection, social isolation and loneliness and treats social connection as a major determinant of health and well-being.

For PushApp, the operational implication is:

> **Do not assume the solution to loneliness is more social volume.**

A useful internal model separates at least:

- **Structure:** enough relationships/interactions/opportunities?
- **Consistency:** do promising contacts actually continue?
- **Function/support:** are there people the user can turn to or share life with?
- **Quality/responsiveness:** does the user feel understood, cared for, respected and meaningfully engaged?

The U.S. Surgeon General's social-connection guidance similarly emphasizes consistent, frequent, high-quality engagement, participation in community groups, support of others, responsiveness and gratitude.

### PushApp translation

The expert should diagnose the **connection bottleneck** before adding social activity.

---

# 3. Relationship quality: responsiveness matters

Close-relationship research on **perceived partner responsiveness** consistently treats feeling understood, cared for and validated by a close other as a central process related to intimacy and relationship quality.

PushApp should not turn that into relationship therapy.

Instead, it can translate the concept into observable, non-clinical behaviors:

- giving attention;
- listening for what matters;
- responding to what the other person actually said;
- expressing appreciation;
- following through;
- sharing time intentionally;
- communicating a need or preference clearly.

### Guardrail

> **Coach the user's contribution to connection; never infer the other person's motives or diagnose the relationship.**

---

# 4. What intervention research does — and does not — justify

Meta-analyses and umbrella reviews of loneliness/social-disconnection interventions show heterogeneous results. Common intervention families include:

- increasing opportunities for social interaction;
- strengthening social support;
- social-skills approaches;
- psychological approaches.

No universal individual-level recipe emerges.

Some psychological interventions target maladaptive social cognition, but that is therapy territory and is not the PushApp lane.

### PushApp translation

Use intervention research to justify:

- matching the action to the connection gap;
- creating repeated opportunities;
- strengthening existing ties;
- supporting follow-through;
- using small experiments;
- evaluating fit and connection quality.

Do **not** build a pseudo-CBT loneliness treatment.

---

# 5. Dating: the expert coaches opportunity and respectful behavior, not attraction

A dating goal contains an unavoidable external outcome: another person has their own preferences, boundaries and right to decline.

Therefore:

### Controllable
- choosing environments where meeting people is plausible;
- showing up;
- initiating respectfully;
- expressing interest clearly;
- following up without pressure;
- learning from fit;
- maintaining one's own boundaries.

### Not controllable
- another person's attraction;
- response speed;
- willingness to date;
- continued interest;
- relationship outcome.

Core rule:

> **Never turn another person's choice into the user's Step.**

The domain explicitly rejects:
- manipulation;
- coercive persuasion;
- “pickup” tactics;
- jealousy tactics;
- repeated pressure after non-interest;
- entitlement to attention, sex, affection or a relationship.

---

# 6. Boundaries and consent

Healthy-relationship resources from CDC/WHO and specialist relationship-safety organizations emphasize communication, respect, boundaries, consent and freedom from coercion/control.

For PushApp:

A boundary is not:
- a tool for controlling another person;
- a threat designed to force compliance.

The useful coaching frame is:
1. What matters to me / what am I comfortable with?
2. What can I communicate clearly?
3. What action is under my control if that limit is not respected?
4. Is there any fear, coercion, threat or safety concern that changes the problem entirely?

### Consent

Consent is ongoing and mutual. Pressure, fear, intoxication-related inability to consent, sexual coercion, or ignored refusal are not ordinary “communication problems.”

They trigger safety/referral logic.

# 7. Loneliness → depression → suicide: escalation logic

Loneliness is associated with depression risk, but PushApp must not diagnose depression from loneliness.

The expert should detect a change of problem class.

## Ordinary connection coaching
“I've been lonely since moving cities and want more people in my week.”

→ Connection framework can continue.

## Mental-health referral threshold
Loneliness occurs alongside persistent or worsening patterns such as:
- sustained low/empty mood;
- loss of interest/pleasure;
- marked withdrawal;
- hopelessness;
- major sleep/appetite/energy changes;
- inability to meet normal responsibilities;
- substantial functional impairment.

→ Recommend mental-health professional support; keep ordinary coaching secondary/light.

## Crisis override
Any direct or strongly suggestive suicide/self-harm signal:
- wanting to die;
- no reason to live / trapped / unbearable pain in a concerning context;
- plan/research/preparation;
- saying goodbye/giving away important items;
- recent escalating suicidal behavior.

→ Stop ordinary relationship coaching and invoke localized crisis/safety flow.

The product should not use loneliness as a proxy suicide screen; it should respond to actual warning signals.

---

# 8. Social fear / avoidance boundary

PushApp may support ordinary initiation friction:

- “I feel awkward sending the first message.”
- “I nearly left the event before walking in.”

It should **not** attempt to treat severe social anxiety.

Referral is appropriate when fear/anxiety:
- is persistent and hard to control;
- produces major avoidance;
- interferes with school, work, relationships or normal daily life;
- causes significant distress over time.

The expert must not diagnose “social anxiety disorder”; it detects out-of-scope impairment.

# 9. Professional bottleneck model

The expert should classify the current problem into one primary bottleneck.

## `STRUCTURE_GAP`
Too few realistic opportunities/interactions.

## `OPPORTUNITY_FIT_GAP`
The user is showing up, but mostly in contexts that do not fit the kind of connection they want.

## `INITIATION_FRICTION`
Opportunities exist but the user repeatedly does not initiate.

## `FOLLOW_THROUGH_GAP`
First contact happens; promising contact does not continue.

## `QUALITY_RESPONSIVENESS_GAP`
People are present, but the user wants greater closeness, attention, support or intentionality.

## `BOUNDARY_CLARITY_GAP`
The user struggles to state preferences, limits or needs in an otherwise non-abusive context.

## `RELATIONSHIP_MAINTENANCE_GAP`
An existing safe relationship needs more consistent investment, repair attempts, shared time or follow-through.

## `OUTCOME_FIXATION`
The user is measuring success primarily by another person's response/attraction/behavior.

## `MENTAL_HEALTH_OR_SAFETY_OVERRIDE`
The current need is no longer ordinary relationship coaching.

Core rule:

> **Diagnose the connection bottleneck before adding connection activity.**

---

# 10. Subtype model

## A. `REDUCE_ISOLATION`
Build enough regular human contact and belonging.

## B. `MEET_NEW_PEOPLE`
Create repeated, fitting opportunities for friendship/community/dating.

## C. `DATING_INITIATION`
Support respectful initiation and follow-through without attraction engineering.

## D. `DEEPEN_CONNECTION`
Increase intentionality, responsiveness or mutual support in a chosen safe relationship.

## E. `MAINTAIN_EXISTING_RELATIONSHIP`
Create repeatable relationship-maintenance behavior.

## F. `ASSERTIVENESS_BOUNDARIES`
Clarify and communicate a need/preference/limit in a safe context.

## G. `RELATIONSHIP_CLARITY`
Help the user clarify their needs, observations and options without issuing a stay/leave verdict.

# 13. Research conclusions for PushApp

1. **Connection is multidimensional; diagnose before prescribing activity.**
2. **Relationship quality is partly behavioral and observable; coach responsiveness, not psychology.**
3. **Repeated-contact contexts are often more useful than one-off “be social” tasks.**
4. **Dating success must be measured by user-controlled behavior, never another person's attraction.**
5. **Boundaries are about the user's limits/actions, not controlling another person.**
6. **Loneliness is not a diagnosis; persistent depressive impairment changes the scope.**
7. **Safety/coercion overrides relationship optimization.**
8. **Severe social fear/avoidance belongs with mental-health care, not forced exposure.**
9. **No attachment-style diagnosis, mind-reading, matchmaking verdicts or therapy protocols.**
10. **The Expert should become less involved as a sustainable real-world connection rhythm develops.**
