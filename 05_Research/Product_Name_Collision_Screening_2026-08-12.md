# Research — Product Name Collision Screening

Status: Preliminary naming screen; not legal trademark clearance.
Date: 2026-08-12.
Scope: Names proposed during the founder/Codex naming discussion. Search covered visible companies,
products, and mobile applications, with extra weight given to personal growth, coaching, wellness,
goals, habits, and social-support products.

## How to read the result

- **Reject** — exact or near-exact active name in a directly overlapping category.
- **High risk** — exact active product/company or many established uses, even if the category differs.
- **Medium risk** — no decisive direct-category collision found in this pass, but the term is crowded,
  descriptive, or has a meaningful near-match.
- **Worth deeper clearance** — no decisive exact app/company collision found in this pass. This does not
  mean the name is legally available.

This research does not search every national company register or trademark office. Any finalist still needs
formal trademark searches by class and territory, App Store/Google Play checks by storefront, domain/social
handle checks, and counsel review.

## Shortlist conclusion

None of the most semantically obvious names is clean enough to adopt based on this first pass.

The two names that justify a second-round investigation are:

1. **MeantTo** — least direct collision found, but weak ownership/searchability and ambiguous spelling.
2. **MeMore** — memorable meaning, but substantial phonetic/visual confusion with `Memore`.

`Toward` also lacks a decisive direct collision in this pass, but is too generic to be a strong recommendation
without a distinctive modifier or invented spelling.

The research suggests that the next round should favor a coined or compound name whose sound and spelling are
distinctive, while preserving one of the desired ideas: becoming, upward movement, self-investment, or mutual
support. A new candidate should be screened immediately before emotional investment in the brand.

## Important product implication

Although **PushApp** is a strong wordplay concept, it is not merely a working name in an empty namespace. An
active technology company already uses the exact name for customer engagement and push notifications—the
literal category most likely to appear in search results. PushApp should therefore remain an internal codename
unless deeper legal analysis produces an unexpected path to safe use.
