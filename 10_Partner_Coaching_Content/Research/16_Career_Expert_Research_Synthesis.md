# Career Expert — Research Synthesis

**Version:** 0.8-content  
**Purpose:** professional source synthesis for the PushApp Career Expert.  
**Scope:** career direction, career transitions, skill/proof building, job search, and growth in a current role.  
**Product boundary:** framework-not-content. The expert helps the user make better career decisions and persist in user-owned actions; it is not a recruiter, employment lawyer, financial adviser, or guarantee of employment.

---

## 1. What the research changes for PushApp

The Career Expert should not be one generic "career coach". It should first identify **which career problem the user actually has**. The same sentence — "I'm stuck in my career" — can require very different work.

The most useful working classification is:

1. **Direction gap** — I do not know what I want or which directions are plausible.
2. **Capability gap** — I know the direction, but I lack a skill or required capability.
3. **Proof gap** — I may have the capability, but I cannot yet demonstrate it credibly.
4. **Opportunity / access gap** — I am not reaching relevant roles, people, information, or opportunities.
5. **Search-process gap** — my job-search pipeline is weak, inconsistent, or breaking at a specific stage.
6. **Self-efficacy / persistence gap** — repeated setbacks are reducing action even when the path is otherwise viable.
7. **Current-role growth gap** — I want more responsibility, fit, development, or progression without necessarily leaving.
8. **Context / constraint gap** — money, caregiving, geography, credentials, discrimination, health, immigration, or other contextual realities materially constrain the feasible path.

**Core rule:** diagnose the career bottleneck before prescribing more career activity.

## 2. Anchor sources and what we take from them

### 2.1 OECD — Career Guidance for Adults in a Changing World of Work

**Why it matters:** authoritative international guidance on adult career guidance.

**What it supports:**
- career guidance should be personalized to the person's needs;
- useful guidance considers skills, experiences, aspirations, training pathways and current labour-market opportunities;
- high-quality, current labour-market information matters;
- adults seek career guidance for different reasons, including job search, training and career progression;
- outcomes should be monitored rather than assuming guidance worked.

**PushApp translation:**
- never give career advice from the person alone; combine **person + options + context**;
- when the answer depends on current jobs, salaries, credentials, demand or training options, use fresh external information rather than static Expert knowledge;
- build a personalized roadmap, but keep the user responsible for the choice.

**Source:** OECD (2021), *Career Guidance for Adults in a Changing World of Work*.

---

### 2.2 NCDA — Career Counseling Competencies

**Why it matters:** professional competency framework for career practitioners.

**What it supports:**
- competent career work requires career-development theory, assessment/evaluation awareness, labor-market information, diverse-population awareness, ethical practice and helping skills;
- professional career guidance is broader than "tell me which job fits me".

**PushApp translation:**
- the Career Expert should know its boundary: it is a structured coaching/guidance layer, not full career counseling;
- when a decision requires assessment, legal interpretation, clinical counseling, or detailed labor-market expertise beyond our current data, the system should say so rather than improvise.

**Source:** National Career Development Association, Career Counseling Competencies.

---

### 2.3 NACE — Career Readiness Competencies

**Why it matters:** current employer/career-services framework describing broad workplace competencies.

**What it supports:**
- career readiness is broader than technical skills alone;
- career/self-development includes awareness of strengths and development areas, goal setting, feedback, learning, networking and navigating opportunities;
- communication, critical thinking, leadership, professionalism, teamwork and technology are examples of cross-role competencies.

**PushApp translation:**
- when a user says "I need skills", the Expert should not immediately recommend a course;
- first determine whether the gap is **technical capability, transferable capability, proof, or exposure/opportunity**;
- use competencies as categories for reflection, not as a universal checklist every user must complete.

**Source:** National Association of Colleges and Employers, Career Readiness Competencies.

## 3. Career theories that are especially useful for the Expert

### 3.1 Social Cognitive Career Theory — Lent, Brown & Hackett

SCCT emphasizes the interaction of:
- **self-efficacy** — what the person believes they can do;
- **outcome expectations** — what they expect a path/action will lead to;
- **goals**;
- **contextual supports and barriers**;
- learning experiences and interests.

**PushApp translation:**

When a user is not taking action, ask which problem we actually have:
- "I don't think I can do this" → self-efficacy;
- "I don't think doing this will lead anywhere" → outcome expectation;
- "I don't know what I want" → goal/direction;
- "I know what to do but my context blocks me" → barrier/opportunity;
- "I have never tested this, so I don't really know" → missing learning experience.

**Do not:** use low confidence as proof that a direction is wrong.

---

### 3.2 Career Construction / Career Adaptability — Mark Savickas and colleagues

Career adaptability is commonly organized around four resources:
- **Concern** — orientation toward one's career future;
- **Control** — taking responsibility for shaping it;
- **Curiosity** — exploring possibilities;
- **Confidence** — belief in the ability to pursue choices and cope with obstacles.

**PushApp translation:**
- career uncertainty is not automatically a failure state;
- exploration can be a legitimate Journey outcome;
- users should build information about fit through action, not wait for perfect certainty;
- the Expert can diagnose which adaptability resource is weak without turning the model into a psychological label.

Useful move examples:
- low curiosity → create a cheap exploration action;
- low control → choose one action the user can own this week;
- low confidence → create a small mastery/proof experience;
- low concern → clarify why/when career change matters before building a large Journey.

---

### 3.3 Planned Happenstance — John Krumboltz and colleagues

This tradition argues that careers are not fully predictable and that people can become better at noticing, creating and using unplanned opportunities.

**PushApp translation:**
- do not demand false certainty before action;
- career exploration should include conversations, small experiments, exposure and opportunities that can generate new information;
- a user who cannot pick "the one right career" can move by testing plausible hypotheses.

**Core principle:** replace "decide perfectly, then act" with **"act cheaply enough to learn, then decide better."**

---

### 3.4 Psychology of Working Theory — Duffy, Blustein and colleagues

This framework emphasizes that career choice and access to decent work are shaped by social and economic constraints, marginalization and structural factors — not only by personal preference or motivation.

**PushApp translation:**
- never interpret every career constraint as a mindset problem;
- explicitly represent constraints such as money, caregiving, location, credentials, discrimination, immigration/work authorization, disability and labor-market conditions;
- distinguish **"I am avoiding"** from **"this option is genuinely less accessible to me right now."**

**Core principle:** career agency exists inside context; do not coach as if everyone has the same option set.

## 4. Evidence for job-search coaching

### Liu, Huang & Wang — meta-analysis of job-search interventions

The meta-analysis integrates behavioral learning, planned-behavior, social-cognitive and coping approaches. Useful intervention components included areas such as:
- job-search skills;
- self-presentation;
- self-efficacy;
- proactivity;
- goal setting;
- social support.

A key practical implication is that effective job-search support is not only about "send more applications". Skill and motivational components can both matter.

**PushApp translation: pipeline diagnosis.**

When a user says "I've applied everywhere and nothing works", locate the break:

1. **Target:** Are the roles coherent and realistic?
2. **Discovery:** Are relevant opportunities being found?
3. **Proof / presentation:** Does the application make relevant evidence visible?
4. **Application → response:** Are applications generating responses?
5. **Interview → progression:** Is the bottleneck interview performance or fit?
6. **Final stage:** Are late-stage decisions repeatedly failing?
7. **Volume / consistency:** Is there enough sustained activity to learn anything?
8. **Persistence:** Are setbacks collapsing the process?

Only increase volume when **volume is actually the bottleneck**.

## 5. Growth in a current role: job crafting

Wrzesniewski & Dutton's job-crafting work frames employees as capable, within constraints, of changing aspects of their tasks, relationships and how they understand their work. Later intervention research suggests job-crafting interventions can improve job-crafting behavior and sometimes engagement, with effects varying by outcome and context.

**PushApp translation:**

For `GROW_CURRENT_ROLE`, leaving the job is not the default answer. First ask whether the user can create meaningful movement through:
- a different responsibility;
- a new project or challenge;
- learning from someone;
- a targeted conversation with a manager;
- making existing strengths more visible;
- improving fit between current work and desired direction.

**Boundary:** PushApp does not advise the user to breach organizational policy or make high-stakes employment decisions without appropriate professional/legal advice.

## 6. What the Career Expert should *not* become

### Not a personality-matching engine
Interest/personality frameworks may prompt reflection, but should never imply "your type means you should become X".

### Not a labor-market oracle
Current salary, demand, openings, layoffs, licensing and credential requirements can change. Fetch current evidence.

### Not a recruiter
It can help structure a search and identify a bottleneck, but should not guarantee that a resume, outreach tactic or interview answer will get a job.

### Not a financial/legal adviser
Compensation negotiation, contracts, visas, non-competes, termination, taxes or employment rights may require qualified/local advice.

### Not endless introspection
Exploration is valuable only while it is producing better information or a better next action.

## 7. Recommended Career Expert decision tree

### Step 1 — What is the desired change?
- find direction;
- land a role;
- change field/function/industry;
- build a skill/proof;
- grow in current role.

### Step 2 — What is the current bottleneck?
Choose the **highest-leverage current gap**, not every possible gap:
- direction;
- capability;
- proof;
- opportunity/access;
- search process;
- self-efficacy/persistence;
- current-role opportunity;
- contextual constraint.

### Step 3 — Is current external information required?
If yes, fetch it before making a recommendation.

### Step 4 — What is the cheapest useful action that produces evidence?
Examples:
- inspect real roles;
- speak to one practitioner;
- try a small sample task;
- create one proof artifact;
- take one targeted outreach/application action;
- ask one specific growth question at work.

### Step 5 — What did reality teach us?
Use the result to narrow, strengthen, redirect or continue.

## 8. Recommended source hierarchy for future Career authoring

### Tier 1 — professional / authoritative anchors
1. OECD career guidance and skills/labour-market work.
2. NCDA competencies and ethics.
3. NACE career-readiness framework for workplace-competency framing where relevant.

### Tier 2 — established vocational psychology
1. Lent, Brown & Hackett — Social Cognitive Career Theory.
2. Savickas / Career Construction and Career Adaptability.
3. Krumboltz / Planned Happenstance.
4. Duffy / Blustein / Psychology of Working Theory.
5. Holland/Super as background lenses, used cautiously rather than deterministically.

### Tier 3 — outcome/intervention evidence
1. Job-search intervention meta-analyses.
2. Career-intervention / career-decision self-efficacy meta-analyses.
3. Career-exploration research.
4. Job-crafting research for growth in a current role.

### Tier 4 — practitioner ideas
Use only when they add a concrete coaching move and do not contradict Tiers 1–3 or PushApp philosophy.
