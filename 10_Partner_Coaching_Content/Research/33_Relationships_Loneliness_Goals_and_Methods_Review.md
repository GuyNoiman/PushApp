# 33 — Relationships / Loneliness: User Goals and Method Review

**Version:** 0.1 research draft  
**Status:** Research and authoring input; not approved for real-user delivery.  
**Domain:** Relationships / Loneliness  
**Stage:** MVP content foundation; clinical and safety approval remains required before release.

## 1. Purpose

This review turns the existing Relationships / Loneliness evidence synthesis into an authoring map:

> user goal → observable bottleneck → suitable method family → candidate Journey.

It does not treat every method below as a PushApp intervention. Some are clinical methods included so
the Expert can recognise the evidence base and its own boundary. PushApp may use only the explicitly
marked non-clinical mechanism, and must refer rather than imitate regulated treatment.

## 2. Research conclusion

There is no defensible universal intervention for “loneliness” or “relationships.” Social connection
has structural, functional and qualitative dimensions, while intervention reviews show heterogeneous
and generally low-certainty effects. The matching unit therefore must not be the domain alone. It is:

> **goal family + primary bottleneck + context + safety status + user constraints.**

The same user statement can require very different Journeys. “I feel alone” may mean too few repeated
opportunities, no follow-through after meeting people, or many contacts without felt responsiveness.
Sending all three users to more events would be an avoidable matching error.

## 3. The seven common user-goal families

The existing seven subtypes remain the right top-level coverage. They are expressed below in the
language of the aspiration, with a user-controlled success condition.

| Goal family | Typical aspiration in the user's language | Observable success owned by the user | Nearest neighbour and separator |
|---|---|---|---|
| `REDUCE_ISOLATION` | “I want people and belonging to be a regular part of my life.” | A sustainable rhythm of repeated contact or participation exists. | Unlike `MEET_NEW_PEOPLE`, the primary gap is regular human connection, not expanding a network for a particular kind of relationship. |
| `MEET_NEW_PEOPLE` | “I want to find people I genuinely fit with.” | The user repeatedly enters fitting contexts and follows up where there is mutual interest. | Unlike `DATING_INITIATION`, the target may be friendship or community and does not require romantic intent. |
| `DATING_INITIATION` | “I want to create a real chance for romantic connection.” | The user creates appropriate opportunities, initiates respectfully and accepts the other person's response. | Unlike `MEET_NEW_PEOPLE`, respectful expression of romantic interest is part of the desired action. |
| `DEEPEN_CONNECTION` | “I want one or more existing relationships to feel more real and supportive.” | The user creates intentional contact and practises voluntary, reciprocal responsiveness. | Unlike `MAINTAIN_EXISTING_RELATIONSHIP`, a relationship exists but depth or mutual support is the central gap. |
| `MAINTAIN_EXISTING_RELATIONSHIP` | “I want to stop letting an important safe relationship fade.” | A repeatable maintenance behaviour is performed and reviewed. | Unlike `DEEPEN_CONNECTION`, the need is consistency, repair or follow-through rather than greater disclosure or closeness. |
| `ASSERTIVENESS_BOUNDARIES` | “I want to say what I need and protect what matters to me.” | The user states a clear request or limit and follows through on the part under their control. | Unlike `RELATIONSHIP_CLARITY`, the desired boundary is broadly known; the difficulty is expressing or enacting it safely. |
| `RELATIONSHIP_CLARITY` | “I want to understand what I need and what my next step should be.” | Observations, needs, unknowns and one user-owned next step are separated without a stay/leave verdict. | Unlike `ASSERTIVENESS_BOUNDARIES`, the user is still clarifying the problem or options. |

### Cross-cutting goal that is not a separate subtype

Recovering after rejection, ghosting or a painful interaction is a persistence need inside several
goal families. It must not become a separate rewardable “failure loop,” and it must not encourage
chasing another person. It changes the next Step size and review, not the user's Dream.

## 4. Method families reviewed

Evidence labels describe confidence for the claimed mechanism, not a promise of individual outcome:

- **Moderate:** supported across systematic reviews or multiple controlled studies, with limitations.
- **Promising:** plausible and supported, but evidence is mixed, indirect or population-specific.
- **Practice-informed:** common structured practice with limited direct outcome evidence for this use.
- **Clinical boundary:** a recognised treatment that PushApp must not deliver as self-guided coaching.

| # | Method family | Mechanism | Best fit | PushApp-safe adaptation | Evidence / boundary |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Connection network mapping | Makes structure, frequency, desired support and neglected ties visible. | Structure, quality and maintenance gaps. | Map relationship circles and choose one gap; never score human worth. | Practice-informed. |
| 2 | Relationship values clarification | Defines what “good connection” means for this user before prescribing activity. | Opportunity fit, depth, boundaries and clarity. | Choose a few relationship qualities and observable signs. | Practice-informed; consistent with values-based action. |
| 3 | Repeated-context design | Repeated exposure to the same people creates continuity opportunities. | `STRUCTURE_GAP`. | Select one accessible recurring group, place or activity and test fit. | Promising; WHO supports community participation, but access alone is not a universal loneliness intervention. |
| 4 | Social prescribing / community linkage | Connects a person to fitting community resources through a navigation layer. | Structure and opportunity-fit gaps. | Search and compare accessible recurring contexts; not a medical prescription. | Promising; WHO says it should not be treated as a stand-alone intervention. |
| 5 | Volunteering / contribution | Combines purpose, repeated contact and reciprocal contribution. | Isolation and belonging where service fits the user. | Test one bounded recurring contribution the user genuinely values. | Promising and context-dependent. |
| 6 | Befriending / peer support | Adds regular contact and support through a bounded relationship or group. | Isolation and support gaps. | Recommend legitimate peer/community programmes; future Ally matching is separate. | Promising; not a substitute for professional care. |
| 7 | Behavioral activation | Reduces avoidance by scheduling small value-aligned actions. | Structure, initiation and follow-through. | One small social action, observe fit, adjust; do not claim to treat depression. | Promising for connection; formal treatment is a clinical boundary. |
| 8 | Graded action ladder | Progresses from a low-friction social action to a harder one. | Ordinary initiation friction. | User chooses voluntary steps and may scale down at any time. | Practice-informed; exposure therapy for severe anxiety is a clinical boundary. |
| 9 | Implementation intentions | Links a chosen action to a concrete cue: “if X, then I will Y.” | Initiation, follow-through and maintenance. | Add one cue and one tiny response to a Journey Step. | Moderate for goal enactment generally; relationship-specific evidence is indirect. |
| 10 | Environment and friction design | Makes appropriate connection actions easier and less dependent on motivation. | Structure and follow-through. | Prepare transport, calendar slot, contact details or a low-pressure draft. | Practice-informed behavior-design adaptation. |
| 11 | Social-skills rehearsal | Practice plus feedback improves specific observable interaction skills. | Initiation friction and ordinary communication gaps. | Rehearse greeting, question, listening or follow-up in the user's own language. | Promising; skills alone do not reliably resolve loneliness. |
| 12 | Conversation initiation practice | Reduces ambiguity around starting without using manipulation. | Meeting people and dating initiation. | Prepare one authentic opener tied to the present context. | Practice-informed subset of social-skills training. |
| 13 | Active listening | Attention, paraphrasing and relevant follow-up increase felt responsiveness. | Deepening and maintenance. | Practise one undistracted conversation and one genuine follow-up question. | Promising; relationship-quality evidence is stronger than intervention evidence. |
| 14 | Gradual self-disclosure | Slightly increases meaningful sharing when safety and reciprocity are present. | Deepening connection. | User selects a voluntary disclosure level and reviews responsiveness. | Promising; must never prescribe vulnerability. |
| 15 | Perceived responsiveness practice | Communicates understanding, care and validation through observable responses. | Depth, support and maintenance. | Listen, acknowledge what matters, ask rather than assume. | Strong relational association; causal intervention evidence is more limited. |
| 16 | Active-constructive responding | Responds with engaged interest when another person shares good news. | Deepening and maintenance. | Ask for one detail and reflect genuine enthusiasm in a culturally comfortable way. | Promising; findings are culturally moderated. |
| 17 | Appreciation / gratitude expression | Makes valued contributions visible and can reinforce maintenance. | Safe existing relationships. | One specific, authentic appreciation; never a forced gratitude quota. | Promising with modest effects; responsiveness is an important boundary condition. |
| 18 | Shared novel activity | Adds self-expansion and fresh shared attention to an existing relationship. | Maintenance and reconnection. | Choose one mutually acceptable, low-stakes new activity. | Promising; not a repair for unsafe or severely distressed relationships. |
| 19 | Relationship maintenance ritual | Turns care into a repeatable behavior rather than an intention. | Maintenance gap. | A short recurring check-in, shared time or follow-through ritual. | Practice-informed; relationship-education evidence is mixed. |
| 20 | Structured request | Separates observation, need and a specific request. | Boundaries and ordinary relationship friction. | Draft the user's own brief request without predicting compliance. | Practice-informed; not couples therapy. |
| 21 | Assertiveness rehearsal | Practises direct, respectful expression while preserving both parties' autonomy. | Boundary-clarity gap. | Rehearse wording, timing and the user's controllable response. | Promising; fear, coercion or retaliation triggers safety flow. |
| 22 | Boundary-to-action planning | Defines what the user will do rather than how another person must behave. | Boundaries. | “If this occurs, I will…” with a safe and realistic action. | Practice-informed; abuse is not a communication problem. |
| 23 | Observations–interpretations separation | Reduces mind-reading and clarifies what is known, inferred and missing. | Relationship clarity and outcome fixation. | Two-column reflection followed by one information-gathering action. | Coaching-safe cognitive reflection; formal CBT is a clinical boundary. |
| 24 | Decision balance / motivational interviewing | Surfaces ambivalence while protecting autonomy. | Relationship clarity and readiness for change. | Coach asks open questions and reflects reasons; Expert returns no verdict. | Moderate across behavior change; relationship application is indirect. |
| 25 | Repair attempt and de-escalation | Interrupts escalation and supports a later return to a solvable issue. | Ordinary conflict in a safe relationship. | Pause, regulate, name intent to return, choose a calmer time. | Practice-informed relationship education; not for coercion or violence. |
| 26 | Collaborative problem-solving | Defines one shared problem and tests a mutually acceptable change. | Safe relationship maintenance. | User prepares one topic and one request; participation by the other person remains voluntary. | Promising through relationship education; effects vary and may fade. |
| 27 | Social identity / belonging activation | Strengthens identification with a valued group rather than contact volume alone. | Isolation and belonging. | Choose a group identity the user values and one authentic participation action. | Moderate but based on a small evidence base. |
| 28 | Mindfulness / self-compassion for social setbacks | Creates room between discomfort and reactive avoidance or chasing. | Rejection, doorway freeze and outcome fixation. | A brief grounding and kind self-response before the next user-owned action. | Weak/mixed loneliness evidence; useful only as support, not the Journey's core treatment. |

## 5. Clinical methods the Expert may recognise but PushApp must not reproduce

The following approaches inform referral and content boundaries, not self-guided Journey templates:

1. formal cognitive behavioral therapy for loneliness or social anxiety;
2. exposure therapy for clinically significant social anxiety;
3. interpersonal psychotherapy;
4. emotionally focused couple therapy;
5. integrative behavioral couple therapy;
6. treatment for depression, suicidality, trauma, coercive control, stalking or violence.

The safe rule is: use a non-clinical behavioral mechanism only when the user remains inside ordinary
coaching scope. Do not rename a therapy protocol and ship it as coaching.

## 6. Goal-to-method portfolio

| Goal family | Primary method candidates | Methods that must not be the automatic default |
|---|---|---|
| `REDUCE_ISOLATION` | Network mapping, repeated-context design, community linkage, volunteering, peer support, behavioral activation, belonging activation | More events without fit; forced social-skills training; pseudo-CBT. |
| `MEET_NEW_PEOPLE` | Values/fit clarification, repeated contexts, friction design, initiation practice, follow-through intentions | Contact quotas; random one-off events; popularity metrics. |
| `DATING_INITIATION` | Fit clarification, opportunity design, respectful initiation rehearsal, graded action, outcome-control review | Pickup tactics, attraction engineering, repeated contact after a no. |
| `DEEPEN_CONNECTION` | Active listening, voluntary self-disclosure, responsiveness, active-constructive responding, appreciation | Forced vulnerability; quantity of messages as depth. |
| `MAINTAIN_EXISTING_RELATIONSHIP` | Maintenance ritual, appreciation, shared novelty, repair attempt, collaborative problem-solving | Conflict skills when there is fear/coercion; assuming mutual participation. |
| `ASSERTIVENESS_BOUNDARIES` | Values clarification, structured request, assertiveness rehearsal, boundary-to-action plan | Controlling rules for another person; negotiation with abuse. |
| `RELATIONSHIP_CLARITY` | Observations vs interpretations, decision balance, values clarification, one information-gathering action | Attachment labels, partner diagnosis, stay/leave verdict. |

## 7. Candidate Journey families for the first authored library

The first release should not turn 28 methods into 28 Journeys. A method is not a user aspiration.
Methods become variants only when the Milestone arc remains the same; a materially different arc is
a separate Journey.

| Goal family | Candidate Journey | Distinct core arc | Likely method axis |
|---|---|---|---|
| `REDUCE_ISOLATION` | Build a Regular Connection Rhythm | Map need → choose repeated context → attend → follow up → sustain | community group vs volunteering vs peer-support context |
| `REDUCE_ISOLATION` | Reconnect With People Already in My Life | map dormant safe ties → choose one → reach out → repeat → review reciprocity | direct reconnection vs shared-activity reconnection |
| `MEET_NEW_PEOPLE` | Find My Kind of Community | define fit → generate contexts → compare → test → choose what to repeat | interest-led vs identity-led vs contribution-led context |
| `MEET_NEW_PEOPLE` | Turn a First Meeting Into Continued Contact | notice fit → make a low-pressure follow-up → create second contact → establish rhythm | message-first vs activity-first follow-through |
| `DATING_INITIATION` | Create Respectful Dating Opportunities | define fit → choose contexts → create opportunities → review | in-person context vs platform-mediated context, if supported later |
| `DATING_INITIATION` | Express Interest Without Pressure | recognise appropriate moment → prepare authentic words → initiate → accept response → learn | planned rehearsal vs tiny in-the-moment action |
| `DEEPEN_CONNECTION` | Make One Friendship More Intentional | choose safe reciprocal tie → create time → listen/share → follow through → review fit | responsiveness-first vs shared-experience-first |
| `DEEPEN_CONNECTION` | Build More Mutual Support | define desired support → make a clear ask → practise responding → create reciprocal rhythm | asking-first vs giving-and-receiving balance |
| `MAINTAIN_EXISTING_RELATIONSHIP` | Restore a Connection Rhythm | name what faded → choose ritual → schedule → perform → adapt | check-in ritual vs shared-time ritual |
| `MAINTAIN_EXISTING_RELATIONSHIP` | Bring Freshness Back to a Safe Relationship | identify stale pattern → choose mutual novelty → try → reflect → repeat/adapt | small novelty vs larger shared project |
| `ASSERTIVENESS_BOUNDARIES` | Say What I Need Clearly | clarify need → draft request → choose moment → communicate → review | direct request vs staged rehearsal |
| `ASSERTIVENESS_BOUNDARIES` | Hold One Boundary Consistently | define limit → define controllable action → communicate → follow through → review safety/respect | reminder/support variant only; no change to core arc |
| `RELATIONSHIP_CLARITY` | Understand What Is Actually Happening | safety check → observations vs interpretations → needs → missing information → one next step | reflection-first vs conversation-first evidence gathering |
| `RELATIONSHIP_CLARITY` | Decide What I Need Before I Decide What to Do | values → trade-offs → non-negotiables → information gap → next step | structured writing vs Coach-guided conversation |

### Recommended first vertical

Author **Build a Regular Connection Rhythm** first. It has a clear controllable outcome, strong fit
with the domain's public-health evidence, low dependence on another person's cooperation, and enough
method variation to test Journey selection without crossing into therapy. The first end-to-end set
after it should cover one depth Journey, one maintenance Journey and one boundary Journey so the
Expert is not silently reduced to “go meet more people.”

## 8. What the matching consultation must learn

The Expert needs only information that can change the recommendation:

1. desired kind of connection;
2. current structure and regularity;
3. where the pipeline breaks: access, initiation, follow-through, quality or maintenance;
4. target context: friendship/community, dating, partnership or another safe relationship;
5. realistic time/access constraints;
6. whether the user wants a staged arc or one recurring practice;
7. safety and clinical-scope override signals handled before ordinary matching.

It does not need the raw conversation, identity, contact names or a full profile. The Coach gathers
missing information and passes closed, allowlisted signals to the Expert.

## 9. Evidence limits that must survive implementation

- Intervention effects are heterogeneous and confidence is often low or very low.
- Association with relationship quality is not proof that prescribing a behaviour will improve every relationship.
- More contact is not always better; fit, consistency, support and responsiveness differ.
- Couple-oriented methods presume a safe context and often some mutual participation.
- Cultural norms change what responsiveness, disclosure and enthusiasm look like.
- Another person's reply, attraction, forgiveness or change can never be a Step completion criterion.

## 11. Decisions and open work

### Approved by existing product principles

- Preserve the seven goal families.
- Match by goal + bottleneck + context, not by domain label alone.
- Measure user-controlled behaviour and perceived fit, never another person's choice.
- Keep clinical methods outside the Journey library.
- Build all Relationships Journeys into the same global Journey registry used by Career.

### Open authoring work

- Write exact closed value ids and negative tests.
- Fully author the first four Journey families with Milestone, Step, cadence, effort and “done when.”
- Produce calibration cases for multiple valid Journeys, one Journey, no match and safety override.
- Obtain formal clinical/safety review and define the minors policy before user exposure.
