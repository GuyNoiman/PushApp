## Result — the six "Personal Growth Styles" (computed from the answers)

**1. Direction**
- *What this means:* You genuinely want to grow, but choosing where to focus is often your biggest challenge. When there are too many possibilities, getting started can feel overwhelming.
- *Strengths:* Curious, open to change, motivated to improve different areas of your life.
- *Biggest challenge:* Deciding what deserves your attention first and committing to one meaningful direction.
- *Remember:* Progress often begins with clarity. Choosing one goal is usually more powerful than pursuing many at once.

**2. Execution**
- *What this means:* Starting isn't usually the difficult part. Your biggest challenge is maintaining consistency over time, especially after interruptions or setbacks.
- *Strengths:* Willing to take action, try new things, and invest effort when engaged.
- *Biggest challenge:* Staying on track when motivation fades or life disrupts your routine.
- *Remember:* Long-term success is built by getting back on track, not by never missing a day.

**3. Planning**
- *What this means:* You usually know what you want to achieve, but turning goals into a practical plan is often your biggest challenge.
- *Strengths:* Motivated to improve; take action once the next step is clear.
- *Biggest challenge:* Large or unclear goals become overwhelming without a structured path.
- *Remember:* You probably don't need more motivation — you need a clearer plan.

**4. Prioritization**
- *What this means:* Your goals matter to you, but daily responsibilities and competing priorities often leave them with less attention than you'd like.
- *Strengths:* Recognize the importance of personal growth and genuinely want to make time for it.
- *Biggest challenge:* Protecting time and attention for your goals when life becomes busy.
- *Remember:* Progress doesn't always require more time — it often requires making your goals a priority.

**5. Self-Efficacy**
- *What this means:* You're motivated by visible progress. Seeing improvement helps you believe your effort is worthwhile and keeps you going.
- *Strengths:* Growth-oriented; enjoy learning, improving, and seeing meaningful results.
- *Biggest challenge:* Staying motivated when progress feels slow or hard to notice.
- *Remember:* Small wins matter. Recognizing steady progress is often what leads to lasting success.

**6. Support**
- *What this means:* You're more likely to achieve your goals when you have encouragement, accountability, or people who support your progress.
- *Strengths:* Open to learning from others; value shared experiences and encouragement.
- *Biggest challenge:* Staying committed is harder when working toward goals entirely on your own.
- *Remember:* The right support system can make difficult goals feel much more achievable.

### How this maps to PushApp (note for the specs)
The six styles are essentially the coach's **friction-diagnosis dimensions**: Direction (what to focus on) ·
Planning (turn into a plan) · Execution/consistency · Prioritization (protect time) · Self-Efficacy (visible
progress) · Support (accountability). They also inform the **unified communication style** (D40) and the
**onboarding questionnaire** (K2) — the onboarding could reuse this instrument to seed both the coach's
opening context and the user's communication-style preference.
